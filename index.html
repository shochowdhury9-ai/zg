The Early Years

Born Zubeen Borthakur in Tura, Meghalaya, he grew up largely in Assam, with deep ties to Jorhat. His father, Mohini Mohon Borthakur, was a government officer and poet who wrote as Kapil Thakur; his mother, Ily Borthakur, was a singer and actor and his first great musical influence.

He was named after the conductor Zubin Mehta. He later took “Garg”, his Brahmin gotra, as his professional surname.

He began singing at three, learned tabla for years under Robin Banerjee, and was introduced to Assamese folk music by Romoni Rai. He gained a Youth Festival gold medal in 1992 for a Western solo performance — the same year Anamika arrived.

Little-known details

He grew up around unusual pets — reportedly a baby tiger, peacocks and a deer — loved football and cricket, and used charity football matches to raise funds through the Kalaguru Artiste Foundation.

The Sound

Folk × Pop

Bihu rhythms and Assamese folk melody carried by pop, rock and electronic production — a sound that felt both rooted and modern.

A Complete Musician

He composed, wrote, arranged, produced, sang and played instruments — closer to an auteur than a conventional playback singer.

Western Harmony, Assamese Soul

Collaborators describe him weaving major, minor, suspended and diminished chords into Assamese arrangements.

Scene Builder

Through his label Zubeen Garg Music he supported younger artists, including Papon and Zublee Baruah.

A Public Conscience

Flood & Covid Relief

Through the Kalaguru Artiste Foundation he helped raise funds for flood-hit communities, and turned his home into a COVID-19 care centre.

Protest & Dissent

He opposed the Citizenship Amendment Act, joined artist-led protests and defended dissent as a democratic right.

Language & Freedom

He insisted music has no language, singing Hindi at Assamese events despite threats.

Nature & Peace

He joined citizens opposing the felling of old trees at Dighalipukhuri, promoted awareness of Majuli, and appealed to ULFA-I to choose education over violence.

The Investigation Singapore & Assam

Please read: Singapore’s State Coroner ruled accidental drowning with no evidence of foul play. Assam’s criminal case is a separate, ongoing trial. Everything in the Assam section is an allegation by the prosecution — not a judicial finding of guilt. All accused pleaded not guilty.

The yacht

The vessel was Crazy Monkey; roughly 15–20 people boarded at Marina at Keppel Bay around 2 pm. The captain said several passengers, including Garg, had been drinking before boarding.

The swim

He first wore a life jacket, removed it as too large, refused a smaller one, and entered the water alone heading towards Lazarus Island. Four others swam after him when he became unresponsive; CPR was given.

Toxicology

Blood alcohol was 333 mg per 100 ml; Singapore’s drink-drive limit is 80 mg. The coroner said this would severely impair coordination and reflexes.

Coroner’s ruling

25 March 2026: accidental drowning. He went into the water voluntarily; no evidence he was forced, that rescuers held him under, or of anaphylaxis. 35 witnesses were heard. Police closed their investigation on 1 April 2026.

Sources differ slightly on timings (e.g. distress call 3:15 pm; hospital arrival 5:35 pm vs a 5:13 pm pronouncement). The family has questioned the delay.

The SIT & chargesheet

After 60+ FIRs, Assam CID formed a Special Investigation Team. Its chargesheet was filed on 12 December 2025 after an 83-day probe: about 2,500 pages (12,000+ with annexures) and nearly 300 witnesses.

Prosecution theory

That he was knowingly exposed to danger: alleged to have epilepsy and prior alcohol de-addiction treatment, yet allegedly supplied alcohol, kept sleep-deprived, encouraged to swim, and not given timely medical help.

Evidence cited

Witness statements, the 333 mg/100 ml ethanol finding, allegedly deleted chats, and an alleged 75-minute ambulance delay said to have cost the “golden hour”.

Financial angle

Allegations of misused festival funds and of misuse of his name/earnings; the family has raised concerns about diverted funds, recovered seals and stamps, and multiple PAN cards.

Charges as framed in May 2026 per public reports. The name of one security officer appears as both “Prabin” and “Paresh” Baishya across sources — verify before publishing.

Legacy

Zubeen Garg made regional identity contemporary. He proved Assamese music could be modern, commercially successful, politically expressive and nationally visible — without losing its language or soul. A year after his death, fans across Assam still gather, sing and remember.
